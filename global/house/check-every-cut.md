description: Cut a speaker on the real end of their line, found in the audio and not in the transcript's word times, and check every cut on its frames before anyone sees it; no next-sentence mouth, no stray frame
metadata:

---

A cut on a speaker must never show them starting the next sentence, and no cut may carry a stray frame of another shot.

- **Find each line's real edges in the audio.** A transcript's word times can run a word long: the last word's end often swallows
  the breath, or even the first sound of the next word ("…paradox, b-"). Take the start and end of speech from the audio's own
  level (the quiet before the first word, the quiet after the last), and cut both sound and picture there.
- **The picture on a speaker ends at their real end of speech.** If the edit needs a pause there, the pause goes to the next
  picture, not to more of the speaker, whose mouth is already moving into what comes next.
- **Check every cut before handing over:** look at the two frames either side of each cut. There should be no mouth starting a new
  sentence and no single frame from a neighbouring shot (a stray frame, often a shot boundary rounded the wrong way). Listen for,
  or re-transcribe to catch, doubled or clipped words at each audio join.
- **When cutting from a file by time, start on the exact frame.** Seek to just before the frame, not just after it, or every
  piece lands a frame late.

**Why:** a reviewer spotted a speaker visibly starting her next sentence before a cut, a doubled "But" where a line ran into the
next word, and stray one-frame flashes of other shots. Each came from trusting transcript times or unchecked cut points.
**How to apply:** in every edit (trailers, reels, long-form), take line edges from the audio, end speaker shots on the real end
of speech, and check every cut on its frames before showing a round.
